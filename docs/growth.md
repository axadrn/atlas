# Growth

This document records the current growth thesis: how Atlas gets from zero
users to the scale where the community loop and the business model start
working. Like every decision document here, it changes when a better idea
wins.

## Why free forever

Atlas is free for users. All of it, including future features. Forever.

This is not generosity, it is strategy. Every platform that became the
biggest of its kind is free for the people who use it: Wikipedia, YouTube,
Reddit, OpenStreetMap. Paywalls cap network effects, and network effects are
the only path to "biggest". The incumbent's paywall is its ceiling and our
opening.

Revenue comes from the other side of the platform, never from users and
never from user data:

- companies pay for the public community data in machine-readable form
  (API access, bulk exports, freshness guarantees), the same data any
  person can read for free on the site;
- employers pay to post jobs in front of the community;
- cities and tourism boards pay for reports on how the community sees them;
- clearly labeled sponsors pay for visibility, never for placement in the
  data;
- open source and public interest funds can bridge the early years.

Personal data is never sold or shared. We sell the phone book that already
hangs in the hallway, in a convenient format. We never sell the diary.

Sponsorship has one hard rule, agreed in writing before any money moves:
sponsorship buys visibility, not truth. Sponsors appear in rankings,
comparisons and community data exactly as if the deal did not exist, and
negative community data about a sponsor is not a termination event.

## The cold start problem

A community platform is worthless to visitor number one. The core loop
(discover, learn, contribute, earn reputation) only works once knowledge
exists, and knowledge only exists once people contribute. Most community
platforms die here, not at monetization.

The answer is single-player value first: tooling that is useful to one
person with zero community, starting with the residency day tracker. Come
for the tool, stay for the network.

## The phases

Nothing here runs in parallel. Each phase earns the next.

### 1. The first 100, by hand

The first hundred users of a community platform are not acquired, they are
known personally. The founder lives inside the target group: colivings,
coworking spaces, nomad meetups. Show the tracker in person, onboard people
one by one, watch where they get stuck, fix it the same evening.

A hundred people who know the founder beat ten thousand anonymous visits.
They forgive bugs, give honest feedback and become the first contributors.

### 2. Launch spikes

Three one-shot channels, fired only when the product delivers a
five-minute wow:

- Hacker News: "Show HN: open source alternative to Nomad List". HN has a
  long record of putting open source alternatives to paid incumbents on the
  front page.
- Product Hunt.
- r/digitalnomad and the large city Facebook groups (Lisbon, Bali,
  Chiang Mai, Medellín), posted honestly as a member of the community, not
  as marketing. "Free forever, built in the open" is its own story in
  groups tired of paywalls and affiliate blogs.

### 3. SEO as the machine

Not a hundred blog posts. Free tools that match search intent:

- Schengen 90/180 calculator;
- 183 day calculator;
- one programmatic page per country, generated from our own data.

Calculator keywords carry large search volume and are poorly served. Every
calculator visitor is one click away from the tracker. This channel
compounds for one to two years and then becomes the largest one.

### 4. The contribution loop

Turn users into residents. The product design for this already exists in
the product document: quests, visible gaps, reputation.

Two rules for seeding:

- present empty pages as invitations, not as failures. "Lisbon has 3 of 12
  data points, you are there, fill one in" converts better than a
  half-empty table that looks broken;
- tracker users are born contributors. A check-in proves presence, so a
  one-tap quest right after it ("what did your flat white cost?") produces
  high-quality observations at almost no friction.

### 5. Multipliers

Nomad podcasts and YouTube channels constantly need guests with a story.
"One person builds an open source counter to Nomad List, free forever" is
one. Ten podcast interviews reach the scene better than any ad budget at
this stage. GitHub itself is a channel: developer nomads are the perfect
early adopters and every star has an amplifier effect.

## Realistic expectations

- first 100 users: two to three months of in-person work;
- 1,000 to 5,000 users: after the launch spikes;
- SEO carries from year two;
- the threshold where sponsors and B2B customers pay seriously, roughly
  50k to 100k monthly visitors: an 18 to 36 month game.

Anyone promising faster is selling something. For building the biggest
platform of a scene, 18 to 36 months is fast.

## What we do not do

- no paid ads before the loop works;
- no growth hacks that trade trust for reach;
- no parallel bets. The order is: single-player tool, first hundred by
  hand, then scale. Nothing skips the line.
